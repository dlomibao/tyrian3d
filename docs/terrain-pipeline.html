HD terrain remastering

How a level's background tiles become HD paintings in the game: from the tiles the map uses, through Gemini, to seam-free atlases the renderer draws, with a setting to switch back.

Inventory→Gemini sheets→Generation in Gemini→ Cutting and seam fixing→Atlas + manifest→Runtime drawing→ Toggle→Checks

Noteworthy: problems we hit and how we solved them

ProblemWhat we did
Tiles are reused in many arrangements. A tile sits beside a dozen different neighbours across the map, so a painting made for one arrangement shows a seam in another. The ground is sent as continuous stretches of the map, so Gemini paints a tile's real neighbours in the same stroke and most edges already continue. The cutter then gives every tile side an edge strip and solves all strips at once (least squares over every pair the map puts side by side, weighted by how often), and moves each tile's border onto its strips over 40 texels. Measured over every edge of TYRIAN's map, the colour step across a tile edge is now about 0.6x the step between texels inside a tile (D58, D59). Where one painting still can't meet a neighbour, the manifest can list neighbour variants, which the game picks from the level's map.
Atlas neighbours bleed in. Bilinear filtering at a tile's edge reads half a texel of whatever is next to it in the atlas, and mipmaps average whole blocks of texels across rect boundaries. Rects sit on a 16-texel grid with at least 16 texels of edge-extended gutter, so mip levels 0-4 never mix two tiles. The shader picks the mip level itself (at most 4) and clamps the read half a texel of the coarser level inside the rect. Each cell shows only its own tile, its edge texels right at the edge. The browser check uses a test atlas with magenta gutters: a frame with no magenta pixels means nothing bled (and widening the rect by 8 texels in the console does turn up magenta, so the check can see a leak).
Palette effects assume palette indices. The level's filters and the smoothies recolour palette indices (D7, D31); a painting has RGB colours. As for the flat sprite art (D42): each texel's brightness gives the shade (0-15) a palette ramp would have, and a hue change or the level filter uses the palette entry of that hue at that shade. Palette animation and swaps can't reach the art.
Partial art. Gemini rarely gets every tile right in one go, and a level should not wait for all of it. The game falls back per tile: a tile missing from the manifest (or of the wrong size, or outside its image, or whose atlas failed to load) draws exactly as before, as the 4x MMPX ground or the original tile.
Gemini copies the pixels. Given a sheet scaled up nearest-neighbour, Gemini often hands back the same square blocks, slightly smoothed, however strongly the prompt says not to. Send a softened sheet (scaled down to the original resolution and back up bicubic): same geometry, no hard blocks to copy. It turned two blocky answers into fully painted ones (section 3).
The best fit is not the best painting. The answer that copied the pixel blocks matched the original best; one painting of a dirt tile had a twig that repeated on all 150 copies. The cutter scores pixel blocks and made-up marks against each candidate and prefers paintings from map regions; the broad shading follows the original (section 4, D59). Look at check/*.jpg for marks that repeat.
Some tiles have no unblocky painting. Every answer for a few tiles (TYRIAN's 39, 110, 332, 333...) kept some of the pixel grid; in game they showed as a rectangle of pixels in the soft ground, around the bunker for instance. The cutter softens the block edges of a chosen painting whose blockiness is above 1.6 (fully from 2.4): a 2.5-texel blur mixed in only around the columns and rows where the 8-texel steps are, so the blocks' insides keep their detail. TYRIAN's worst tiles went from 2.2-2.8 to about 1.6 (D60).
The first frame paid for the atlases. Decoding and uploading TYRIAN's atlases with their mipmaps took ~85 ms on the level's first frame, which was enough to push enemies whose models were still loading past the 250 ms hold (D54), so in ?debug=play&skip=2000 the worm came out as extruded sprites. The level preload uploads the atlases to the GPU (renderer.initTexture) before the level starts. The first frame now costs the same in both looks (~180 ms with models, in the harness) (D60).
A dev server answers a missing file with the page. Fetching a missing manifest returned index.html. The loader treats anything that doesn't parse as a manifest as "no art for this tileset".

The goal

The ground, the translucent ice clouds and the floating islands of a level are drawn from HD paintings of the original tiles, at 8 texels per original pixel, where they exist. They must keep the original's layout, shapes and colours (an enemy standing on an island's edge must still be on it), meet seamlessly in every arrangement the map uses, and keep the 3D structure models (D34, D47) as they are. The Options screen switches between the HD terrain and the terrain as it was. Anything we make from the tiles may be public, credited to the original artists in CREDITS.md; the original data never is (D3, D41).

1. Inventory

python tools/terrain_hd.py inventory --level 1-9 --work DIR

Every tile the level's map draws on layers 0, 1 and 2 (layer numbers as in the game's draw list: 0 ground, 1 the blended background 2, 2 background 3), leaving out tiles a structure model replaces. Each tile gets its usage count, usual neighbours, a representative 3x3 neighbourhood and a material group (forest, dirt, road, cloud, rock...). Structure models are matched on the whole map as the game matches them (biggest pattern first): layer 1-2 models replace their tiles, ground models keep them (they are still drawn under the model). For TYRIAN (map 1-9, shapesz.dat) that leaves 66 ground tiles (4,200 cells, 29 of the tiles under trees, bunkers and boulders), 15 cloud tiles (3,208 cells) and no layer-2 tiles: every island cell is under a model. npm run tiles writes the per-tile references in reference/tiles/<shapes>/ (D34), and map-1-9-l{0,1,2}.png show each layer whole.

2. Gemini sheets

python tools/terrain_hd.py sheets --work DIR

PNGs on black, padded to an aspect ratio Gemini keeps (1:1, 3:4, 4:3, 9:16, 16:9), each with its prompt. For the ground, 7x6-tile stretches of the map at 6x (1080 px), chosen as a set cover so every tile sits inside one away from its border (10 sheets for TYRIAN). For the clouds, 3x3 contexts (the tile with its usual neighbours) at 4x, 7-9 per sheet (2 sheets). Ground contexts by material are made too, as optional retries (9 sheets). DIR/sheets/manifest.json lists them with their prompts and says where every tile sits. The sheets are rendered from the game data, so they stay out of git.

3. Generation in Gemini

Sheets are sent to Google Gemini in the user's Chrome, one request at a time (Gemini rate-limits). Answers are saved as DIR/results/<sheet>.png (any size, same aspect as the sheet).

What we used for TYRIAN (1-9). The "Create images" page (gemini.google.com/images, Nano Banana) with the Pro model, one new chat per sheet, driven by Claude in Chrome. 21 sheets plus 6 retries, 27 requests in about 40 minutes; each answer took 15 s to 3 min. No rate-limit error came up, but after about 25 requests Gemini showed "You're running low on usage. The Pro model can use it up faster.", so plan a level's sheets within roughly that budget (or switch to Flash).

  1. Put the sheet in. Copy the PNG to the macOS clipboard: osascript -e 'set the clipboard to (read (POSIX file "SHEET.png") as «class PNGf»)', focus the prompt box, press ⌘V and wait about 6 s for the upload. The clipboard is shared, so one sheet at a time. Gemini shows the upload at 1024 px or the sheet's own size; either is fine.
  2. Aspect ratio. Click the "Aspect ratio" button under the prompt and the sheet's ratio from manifest.json (1:1 for every sheet but ctx-l0-brown-modelled-2, 4:3). Doing this from page JavaScript works: find the button whose label starts with "Aspect ratio", click it, then click the menu entry whose text is the ratio.
  3. Prompt. The sheet's prompt from manifest.json. Inserting it with document.execCommand('insertText', false, prompt) on the focused [contenteditable=true] box is instant; check the box's innerText.length equals the prompt's length and that the page text contains the ratio, then press Return.
  4. Wait. Poll from page JavaScript in calls under 40 s (the browser bridge times out at 45 s) for an img inside model-response with naturalWidth ≥ 1000 (the preview is 1024 px; the uploaded sheet sits in user-query, so it isn't mistaken for the answer). Watch the body text for "something went wrong" / "hard time" / "limit". Sometimes the URL stays on /images for a minute before the chat appears: keep polling rather than resubmitting.
  5. Bring it back. Click the answer's "Download full size image" button (from JavaScript: the last model-response button[aria-label="Download full size image"]), about 3 s after the image appears (an immediate click once did nothing). It saves ~/Downloads/Gemini_Generated_Image_*.jpeg at 2048x2048 (2400x1792 for 4:3), twice the on-page preview. Move it to DIR/results-raw/<sheet>.jpeg, save as DIR/results/<sheet>.png; no padding crop was needed as every answer kept its sheet's aspect.
  6. Check. A side-by-side with the sheet, a 600 px crop at full size (that is where pixel copying shows), and a quick normalised cross-correlation of the answer against the sheet over scale 0.90-1.15 and shift: every answer registered at scale 1.00, shift 0, so Gemini kept the geometry on all of them.

For the next level: send the softened sheets from the start (they cut the pixel copying, without moving anything the cutter registers against); say the material and the viewpoint in the prompt for anything with height (trees, ridges), since "top-down" alone gets trees drawn from the side; check every answer at full size, not just the thumbnail.

Tips that carried over from the sprite art (D43): send the image by putting the PNG on the clipboard and pressing ⌘V rather than pasting image data through the agent; allow automatic downloads in Chrome for the Gemini site; wide strips are refused, so keep sheets close to a supported aspect ratio. python tools/terrain_hd.py fake --work DIR makes stand-in answers (MMPX, blurred, shifted, colour-shifted) to test the rest of the flow without Gemini.

4. Cutting and seam fixing

python tools/terrain_hd.py cut --work DIR [--results DIR/results] [--out public/art/terrain]

Each answer cell is registered to the sheet's picture of it (normalised cross-correlation over a rescale of ±6% and a shift, then each tile by a small shift), every tile is resampled from the registered art at 8x and colour-matched to the original tile, and one candidate per tile is chosen (below). Layers 1 and 2 take their alpha from the original silhouette, upscaled with MMPX among the tile's usual neighbours (index 0 is transparent), with its contour smoothed at one original pixel: the mask's signed distance, blurred at 8 texels, with a 2+2-texel ramp across zero, so the 8-texel stairs MMPX leaves on shallow slopes come out as curves (D67).

Seams (D58). Every tile side gets an edge strip (its outer 4 texels, smoothed along the edge). All strips are solved at once: least squares over every pair of tiles the map puts side by side, weighted by how often, with a pull of 0.15 towards the tile's own painting. Each tile's border is then moved onto its strips, the difference fading in over 40 texels, so the painting's detail stays. Alpha is not averaged (it would draw a faint line of cloud where one side is empty); instead the smoothed distance under it is solved the same way, over the pairs the map has at least 5 times, so contours of tiles that aren't each other's usual neighbours (43|44, 63|64) meet without a jog or sliver (D67). terrain_hd.py --work DIR realpha redoes only this alpha on the committed atlas, keeping its colour (it needs DIR/inventory.json, not the answers).

Choosing among a tile's paintings (D59). A tile usually appears in several regions and contexts, so it has several candidate paintings. The best fit to the original is not the best painting: on TYRIAN the answer that copied the pixel blocks fit best. The score is the fit (NCC) minus the colour error, plus:

  • pixel blocks: steps between texels that line up with the original pixel grid (ratio ~1 painted, 3-4 copied) cost above 2.0;
  • made-up marks: detail the original doesn't have (a twig, a crack) costs, relative to the tile's other candidates: one mark repeated on every copy of a dirt tile makes a grid across the map;
  • regions over contexts: a region's painting shares its neighbours' texture, a context's may not.

Then a chosen painting that still has pixel blocks above 1.6 has its block edges softened (a 2.5-texel blur around the 8-texel grid lines only; --deblock-from 0 turns it off, D60), and the painting's broad shading (blur at 16 texels) is replaced by the original's, so a tile doesn't show a darker corner every time it repeats. cut-report.json lists each tile's source and scores; --exclude 0:332 leaves a tile out (it falls back), --pick 0:68=region-l0-03#0 forces a candidate, and --cache FILE keeps the registered candidates between runs (registration takes minutes).

5. Atlas and manifest

One atlas per layer that has art, beside one manifest per tileset:

public/art/terrain/<tileset>/manifest.json
public/art/terrain/<tileset>/<layer>.webp

{ "version": 1, "scale": 8,
  "layers": { "0": { "image": "0.webp",
                     "tiles": { "123": { "x": 16, "y": 16, "w": 192, "h": 224 } },
                     "variants": { "123": [ { "n": { "right": 124 }, "x": 224, "y": 16, "w": 192, "h": 224 } ] } } } }
  • <tileset> is the shapes file without .dat (shapesz); a tile key is the tile's 0-based record in it (the number in reference/tiles/…/tiles.json and after the colon in the game's tile id).
  • Every rect is the tile times scale (192 x 224 at 8x), at a multiple of 16 texels, with at least 16 texels of edge-extended gutter. Atlases are at most 4096 x 4096; a rect may name its own image.
  • Layer 0 is opaque. Layers 1 and 2 are RGBA with straight alpha and colour bled under alpha 0. Cloud alpha is coverage only: the game applies the 55% translucency itself (D45).
  • variants are optional: the first whose listed neighbours (tile numbers, or -1 for none) all match the map is used.

6. Runtime

Files: src/render/remaster/terrainArt.ts (manifest, per-tile lookup, texel maths), terrainLoader.ts (fetching), terrainMaterial.ts (shaders), and the hooks in drawListRenderer.ts.

  • Loading. When a level starts, preloadLevel requests its tileset's manifest and atlases and waits for them with the models (D54), so the ground doesn't change look during play; with the HD look on, uploadTerrain then sends the atlases to the GPU so the first frame doesn't pay for it (D60). Harnesses that skip the preload (?debug=sprites, &preload=0) start the load at the first tile drawn. No manifest, a bad one, or a failed atlas just means those tiles draw as before.
  • Drawing. Only in the remastered backgrounds (the default; ?bg=original keeps the original tiles). A tile with art is a flat quad in the same place and size as the original tile (D6), one instanced draw per atlas and kind: ground (opaque, lit, a little relief from the painting's brightness, receives shadows), blend (the clouds, translucent at 55% of the art's coverage) and cutout (islands without a model: alpha cut at one half, writing depth and casting island-shaped shadows). Structure models still win over the tiles they replace. Any tile without art goes on to the 4x MMPX ground, and the original tile if that can't draw.
  • Sampling. The shader computes the mip level from the texel footprint (at most 4), clamps the texel position half a texel of the coarser blended level inside the rect, and reads with textureLod.
  • Palette effects. The smoothies' hues (lava, ice, water, including water's ripple mark) and the level filter's hue and brightness recolour the art by brightness. Not on the art: palette animation and swaps, and water's exception for grey and cloud-blue indices (it recolours all of the art).

7. Toggle

Options → Graphics → Terrain: Original / HD remaster switches at once and is kept with the other settings. ?terrain=original|hd overrides it for the page. HD is the default; "Original" means the terrain as it was before the HD art (the 4x ground). In ?debug=play and the other harnesses, tyrianHarness.view.draws.setTerrainStyle("original") switches in the console, and tyrianHarness.view.draws.stats().hdTiles counts HD tiles drawn this frame. ?terrainart=art/other/ loads the art from another folder under public/ (for a test atlas).

8. Checks

  • python tools/terrain_hd.py check --work DIR: the level's map rebuilt from the atlas, original beside HD, and a seam measure (the colour step across tile edges against the step between texels inside tiles).
  • tests/render/terrainArt.test.ts: manifest parsing, per-tile fallback, variants, the loader's failures, and seam-free sampling on a CPU model of the GPU's trilinear filtering over an atlas with magenta gutters, at every mip level the shader may use.
  • In the browser: play TYRIAN with ?debug=play&seed=1&skip=T&freezeAt=T for T = 30, 300, 1000, 2000, 3000, 4200 and 5500 (the clouds, the dirt and ridges, the worm, the rock fortresses, the grass and pools, the end), each against &terrain=original; try a low &dpr=0.3 (far mips) and look along tile edges. The level filter and smoothies show at the level's events.
  • Fallback: copy the atlas folder under public/ with a tile deleted from the manifest and open &terrainart=<folder>/: that tile's cells draw as the 4x ground, nothing else changes, and stats().hdTiles drops by its count.
  • Switching: Options → Terrain in the game, and from the harness console (await import("/src/core/terrainStyle.ts")).setTerrainStyle("original") (live, no reload).
  • Performance: the overlay's fps and cpu ms with and without HD (TYRIAN: 120 fps, ~2 ms either way; the first frame ~180 ms in both, models included).

$PY tools/terrain_hd.py --level 1-9 --work $W check                        # $W/check/*.jpg, seams.json

Extending to another level or tileset

  1. TILES_SHAPES=<x> npm run tiles for its tileset's references.
  2. terrain_hd.py inventory --level <ep>-<n> with a new work folder, then sheets, Gemini (softened sheets from the start, section 3), cut and check. The tileset comes from the level; the atlas goes to public/art/terrain/<tileset>/.
  3. Levels that share a tileset share its atlas. The tool inventories one level at a time, so cutting a second level of the same tileset replaces the first's atlas: until the tool can inventory several levels together, merge the inventories by hand or cut the tileset from the level that uses the most of it.
  4. Look at the contact sheets and cut-report.json (NCC under ~0.5: the cell moved or was repainted; blockiness over 2: retry the sheet softened; a mark that repeats: --pick another candidate).
  5. Play the level in both looks (section 8). Nothing in the game needs to change: it loads the tileset's manifest when the level starts.

Known limits

  • Fades and palette tricks. The art is recoloured by brightness (D42's method). The level-start fade (brightness -14 to 0 over ~14 ticks) darkens it more than the original, whose darkest ramp entries keep some colour (the cloud blue's darkest is still blue): for that half second the HD clouds look grey and the ground black. Palette animation and swaps don't reach the art.
  • Partial art. Where a tile has no art, it draws as the 4x MMPX ground: correct, but a seam of style against painted neighbours.
  • Weak tiles on TYRIAN (D59, D60): the forest canopy (210-233) keeps some checker, mostly under the tree models; the cloud rims (40-44, 50-52) are puffier than the original; a few rare vertical cloud pairs (42 over 52, 42 over 61, 44 over 53) keep a visible step; tiles 39, 110 and 330-333 are soft where Gemini copied blocks.
  • Resolution. Gemini's full-size answers are 2048 px (2400 for 4:3): about 11 painted pixels per original pixel on the ground regions (6x sheets of 1080 px) but only about 7.4 on the cloud contexts (4x, 1104 px), so the clouds' 8x is slightly upsampled.
  • Original background mode. HD applies only to the remastered backgrounds; ?bg=original is the raw tiles whatever the Terrain setting.

Decisions: D34 (remastered backgrounds), D42-D43 (flat sprite art from Gemini), D45 (blended clouds), D48 (remastered by default), D57-D60 (HD terrain).
