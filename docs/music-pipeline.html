Music and sound remastering

How the original FM soundtrack becomes the Synth and Orchestral versions, and how the sound effects and voices are remade.

music.mus (game data)→MIDI + analysis→Arrangement script→ Render, mix, master→Report + listening→Manifest→Game

Noteworthy: problems we hit and how we solved them

ProblemWhat we did
Getting the notes right. The songs are FM chip instructions, not sheet music; parsing the file misses what the player does at runtime (arpeggios, slides, vibrato, drum pitch falls). Record what the game's own player writes to the FM chip, so the MIDI is exactly what you hear. Pitch movement becomes pitch bend, and pitches are the fundamental each FM patch really sounds, not the raw register value.
Echoes look like extra parts. Tyrian's player often doubles a line on another channel a few ticks later and quieter. src.echoes() finds those pairs so an arrangement plays them as a delay or reverb on one instrument, not as a second, clashing instrument.
Seamless loops with reverb. A loop rendered once ends with reverb and releases cut off, and starts dry. Render the loop twice and keep the second pass: its start already holds the tails of the previous pass, so the file is exactly one loop long and loops cleanly.
Real instruments have limited ranges. Samples are silent outside their recorded notes. check_ranges warns about silent notes; Part.extend stretches the outermost samples; split_by_range hands a line between instruments by register.
Checking dozens of songs by ear alone is slow. Each render writes a report comparing the original's and the new version's spectrograms and loudness over time, so structural mistakes (a missing section, a wrong loop point, a part far too loud) show at a glance.
8-bit sound effects at 11 kHz are hissy and dull, and simply upsampling keeps that. Resynthesize instead: analyse tones and noise, rebuild them cleanly at 48 kHz, and extend the high end. Match each original's perceived loudness so the game's mix doesn't change. The originals were clipped near full scale, so the files stay below it and tell the game how much gain to add back.
Voices had to sound like the same ship computer saying the same lines in the same time. Text to speech at the speaking rate that best matches each line's length, time-stretched and pitch-shifted with formants preserved, then a ship-computer effects chain.
Private work and a public game. The remaster started as a private repo while the rights were unclear. Local builds serve the private pack at the same path as the public one and take priority; the public build ships only what the person decided to publish. When the private folder is missing, requests fall through to the public copy instead of failing. The rights (the soundtrack is sold as all rights reserved through Materia Collective) were researched and reported before publishing; the person decided to publish the covers with credit (D40).

0. Setup (once, macOS)

cd ../tyrian3d-remaster
tools/setup.sh      # Python venv (numpy, scipy, soundfile, mido, pedalboard, matplotlib), builds sfizz_render,
                    # downloads the CC0 sample libraries VSCO-2-CE and VCSL into samples/

1. The original, as notes

The game plays music.mus (41 songs in Loudness format) through a port of OpenTyrian's player and an OPL2 FM emulator: that's the Original music style. To arrange a song we need it as notes, exactly as the game plays it:

MIDI_OUT=../tyrian3d-remaster/source npm run songs-to-midi

This runs each song through the same player and records what it writes to the FM chip (tests/tools/ldsToMidi.test.ts):

  • song-NN.mid: one track per OPL channel (plus a drum track for rhythm mode). One MIDI tick is one player update (69.5 Hz). A note starts where the player strikes one. Slides, vibrato, scoops and drum pitch falls become pitch bend; pitches are the fundamental each FM patch actually sounds; velocity follows the carrier's level.
  • song-NN.json: length, loop point, row speed, and per channel the note range, count and FM patches.
  • original/song-NN.ogg: the original as the game plays it (one loop), for comparison.

2. Arranging a song

Each song has one script per style: arrangements/orchestral/song-NN.py and arrangements/synth/song-NN.py. Its docstring is the analysis: sections and bars, which channel plays what, echoes, tuning, and the arrangement chosen. The script assigns the original's lines to instruments with tools/remaster.py:

src = load_source(NN)                         # the original, as notes per OPL channel
lead = src.channel(2)
parts = [
    Part("violins", lead, "sfz:VSCO-2-CE/Strings/1st Violins Sustain.sfz", gain_db=-3, pan=-0.3),
    Part("bass", src.channel(0), {"osc": "saw", "filter": {"cutoff": 400}}, transpose=-12),
]
render_song(NN, "orchestral", parts)
  • Orchestral parts use SFZ sample instruments (VSCO-2-CE, VCSL; both CC0), rendered by sfizz. Samples only sound within their recorded range (tools/INSTRUMENTS.md). Part.extend stretches the outermost samples, split_by_range hands a line from violins to violas to cellos by register, and check_ranges warns about notes that would be silent.
  • Synth parts use presets of tools/synth.py, a small offline synthesizer: saw, square, pulse, triangle, supersaw, FM and noise oscillators, a resonant filter with envelope, glide, vibrato, sub oscillator and synthesized drums. Nothing third-party is used.
  • Faithful to the original. Every note is played as the original strikes it and held as long as it's held, with its pitch movement (scoops, vibrato, drum falls) as bends. Tyrian's player often doubles a line on a second channel a few ticks later and quieter (an echo); src.echoes() finds these so the arrangement can turn them into a delay or reverb instead of a second instrument. Added parts (pads, risers) are tuned to the original, which can sit slightly flat.

In this project the scripts were written with Claude agents, one song and style at a time, from the analysis and by listening against the original.

3. Rendering

.venv/bin/python arrangements/orchestral/song-30.py

render_song renders every part, mixes them with pan, gain and a shared reverb, masters the mix to a target level (master_db, -14 by default) and writes:

  • out/<style>/song-NN.ogg: the song. A looping song is exactly one loop long and loops seamlessly: it's rendered twice and the second pass is kept, so the reverb tails and releases wrap around.
  • out/<style>/song-NN.report.png: spectrograms of the original and the new version, and their loudness over time.
  • out/<style>/song-NN.json: file, title, seconds, loops.

4. Checking it

  • Read the report: the new spectrogram should follow the original's structure (sections, drops, the loop point), and loudness over time should rise and fall where the original does.
  • Listen side by side: tools/export_listen.sh NN writes WAV and MP3 of the original and every rendered style to listen/.
  • Check the loop: play across the loop point in the game; there should be no click or gap.

5. Into the game

.venv/bin/python tools/build_manifest.py   # collects the .json files into out/manifest.json
  • Local builds (npm run dev, npm run play:local) serve the private out/ folder at tyrian-remaster/ (TYRIAN_REMASTER overrides the path).
  • To publish, copy the .ogg files and the manifest into public/tyrian-remaster/ in this repo. The deployed site then offers them too; when the private folder is missing, local builds fall through to the public copy.
  • In game, M cycles Original, Orchestral and Synth (also in Options; ?music=orchestral picks one). The choice is remembered in the browser; until one is made the game starts on Synth (D38). Switching replays the current song in the new style.

Sound effects and voices

.venv/bin/python tools/sfx.py [ids] [--listen]    # macOS; reads the originals from the game data (TYRIAN_DATA)

All 38 sounds (29 effects from tyrian.snd, 9 voices from voices.snd, originally 8-bit mono at 11 kHz) are remade (D36). The originals are only read, never copied.

  • Effects are resynthesized. Tonal parts become sinusoidal tracks played on clean oscillators; noisy parts become band-following noise extended above 5.5 kHz. An exciter adds overtones, and some sounds get designed layers (sub-bass drops and debris under explosions, onset transients, three CC0 percussion samples). Each keeps its original's pitch, timing, length and loudness. Files peak at -0.3 dBFS, and the manifest's gain_db tells the game how much to add back.
  • Voices are spoken by macOS say (Samantha), timed to the original's length, pitched to match, and given a ship-computer treatment (EQ, compression, a quiet vocoder buzz, a short comb and a small room).
  • Each sound writes out/sfx/remaster/NN-name.ogg with a report and metrics. In game, Options → Sound style → Remaster (or ?sound=remaster) switches to them; the original sounds stay the default.
